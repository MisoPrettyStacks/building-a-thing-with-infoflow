Methodology, mathematics and theory

0. What is being forecast, and what “accurate” means here

Let $P_t$ be the XRP/USD price at the close of a 5-minute bar, $h=3$ bars, and $S_t=\ln(P_{t+h}/P_t)$ the 15-minute log return. The forecast is $p_t=\Pr(S_t>0\mid\mathcal F_t)$ where $\mathcal F_t$ contains only bars that had already closed at time $t$. The outcome is $y_t=\mathbf 1[S_t>0]$ (exact ties are excluded and counted).

Accuracy is judged with strictly proper scoring rules, which a forecaster maximises in expectation only by reporting its true belief (Brier 1950; Gneiting & Raftery 2007). Short-horizon crypto prices are close to a martingale, so a forecaster that claims much more than a sliver of skill will be punished by the Brier score. The reference is the no-skill forecast $p=\tfrac12$ with $BS=0.25$. The design goal is therefore: be calibrated first, extract whatever small, real skill exists second, and never be more confident than the live record justifies.

1. Data

  • Source: Coinbase Exchange public candles for XRP-USD, 300-second granularity, closed bars only (a bar is used only after $t_{\text{start}}+300\le$ now). No keys, no paid feeds, no synthetic or placeholder values anywhere in the pipeline.
  • Missing bars (no trades) are filled with the previous close and zero volume; the number of filled bars is published.
  • Kraken and Bitstamp spot prices are fetched each cycle as an independent cross-check; the divergence is published.
  • Both the forecast and its resolution use the same venue, so a basis between exchanges cannot leak into the score.

2. Causal features

Volatility is an exponentially weighted moving average of squared 5-minute log returns $r_t=\ln(P_t/P_{t-1})$ (RiskMetrics, J.P. Morgan 1996):

$$\sigma_t^2=\lambda\,\sigma_{t-1}^2+(1-\lambda)\,r_t^2,\qquad \lambda=0.94 .$$

Every feature is dimensionless (scaled by volatility) and winsorised at $\pm6$:

$$z_{k,t}=\frac{\ln(P_t/P_{t-k})}{\sigma_t\sqrt k},\ k\in\{1,3,12,36,144,288\},\qquad \mathrm{dev}_{n,t}=\frac{\ln P_t-\mathrm{EMA}_n(\ln P)_t}{\sigma_t\sqrt n},\ n\in\{12,48\},$$ $$\mathrm{volsign}_t=\operatorname{sign}(r_t)\cdot\operatorname{clip}\!\Big(\frac{\ln(1+V_t)-m_t}{s_t},-4,4\Big),$$

with $m_t,s_t$ an exponentially weighted mean and standard deviation of $\ln(1+V)$ (decay 0.99) computed before bar $t$ is included. The set of features in use is itself a tunable part of the configuration.

3. Four base models

  1. Climatology: $b_t=\lambda_b b_{t-1}+(1-\lambda_b)y$, with $\lambda_b=0.999$. This is also the scoring baseline.
  2. Online logistic regression: $p=\sigma(w^\top x_t)$ trained by AdaGrad (Duchi, Hazan & Singer 2011) on the log-loss gradient $g=(p-y)x+\ell_2 w$:   $G_k\leftarrow G_k+g_k^2,\; w_k\leftarrow w_k-\eta\,g_k/(\sqrt{G_k}+\epsilon)$.
  3. Drift model: $p=\Phi\!\big(\hat\mu_t\sqrt h/\sigma_t\big)$, $\hat\mu_t$ an EWMA of returns (decay 0.999) that starts at 0, so it is shrunk toward a martingale by construction.
  4. Short-horizon model: a 3-parameter logistic regression on $(z_1,z_3,1)$ capturing bid-ask-bounce and very short momentum.

4. Pooling, calibration and shrinkage

The members are combined by log-odds (geometric) pooling with simplex weights learned by the Hedge algorithm with fixed share (Freund & Schapire 1997; Herbster & Warmuth 1998):

$$z_t=\sum_{k=1}^{4}w_{k}\,\mathrm{logit}(p_{k,t}),\qquad \tilde w_k=\frac{w_k e^{-\eta\,\ell_k}}{\sum_j w_j e^{-\eta\,\ell_j}},\qquad w_k\leftarrow(1-\alpha)\,\tilde w_k+\frac{\alpha}{4},$$

where $\ell_k$ is member $k$'s log-loss on the newly revealed outcome. Fixed share lets the weights recover quickly when the best member changes (regime change). The pooled log-odds are then recalibrated online with Platt scaling (Platt 1999; Guo et al. 2017):

$$\tilde p_t=\sigma(a z_t+b),\quad a\in[0,1.5],\ b\in[-0.2,0.2],$$

updated by stochastic gradient descent on log-loss. Finally a shrinkage factor $\lambda_s\in[0,1]$, set by the agent from live evidence (section 8), gives the published probability

$$p_t=\operatorname{clip}\!\big(\tfrac12+\lambda_s(\tilde p_t-\tfrac12),\,0.02,\,0.98\big).$$

5. Full predictive distribution (intervals that agree with the probability)

The 15-minute return is modelled as a location-scale Student-t, $S=s\,(\mu'+\varepsilon)$, $\varepsilon\sim t_\nu$, with $\mu'=T_\nu^{-1}(p_t)$. Then $\Pr(S>0)=T_\nu(\mu')=p_t$ exactly, so the direction probability and the quantile forecasts can never contradict each other. The scale is calibrated from realised standardised returns $u_j=S_j/(\sigma_j\sqrt h)$:

$$m_2\leftarrow\lambda_k m_2+(1-\lambda_k)u^2,\quad m_4\leftarrow\lambda_\kappa m_4+(1-\lambda_\kappa)u^4,\quad \kappa=\frac{m_4}{m_2^2},\quad \nu=4+\frac{6}{\kappa-3}\ (\text{clipped to }[3.5,30]),$$ $$s_t=\frac{\sqrt{m_2}\;\sigma_t\sqrt h}{\sqrt{\nu/(\nu-2)}},\qquad q_\tau=s_t\,\big(\mu'+t_{\nu,\tau}\big),\ \tau\in\{.05,.10,.25,.50,.75,.90,.95\}.$$

The kurtosis relation uses the excess kurtosis $6/(\nu-4)$ of $t_\nu$. The $t$ CDF and quantiles are computed from the regularised incomplete beta function implemented in lib/stats.js and checked against published tables in the self-test.

6. Learning protocol and the no-leakage guarantee

The label of the forecast made at bar $j$ becomes known only when bar $j+h$ closes. At bar $i$ the engine first learns from $j=i-h$ (never later), then forecasts. Forecast and backtest use the single function walkForward(), so there is no separate research path that could differ from production. scripts/selftest.js mutates every bar after time $T$ and asserts that all forecasts and quantiles up to $T$ are bit-identical; it also checks that the engine reaches the exact oracle score on a planted autocorrelated series and scores about 0.25 on pure noise (it does no harm).

7. Scoring statistics shown on this page

  • Brier score $BS=\frac1n\sum_i(p_i-y_i)^2$; skill $BSS=1-BS/BS_{\text{ref}}$ against $0.25$ and against the climatology member. Confidence interval: moving-block bootstrap (Künsch 1989), block length 12, 1000 resamples, fixed seed.
  • Log loss $-\frac1n\sum[y\ln p+(1-y)\ln(1-p)]$.
  • Reliability: equal-count bins; $\mathrm{ECE}=\sum_b\frac{n_b}{n}|\bar y_b-\bar p_b|$; Murphy (1973) decomposition $BS\approx\mathrm{REL}-\mathrm{RES}+\mathrm{UNC}$ with $\mathrm{UNC}=\bar y(1-\bar y)$; and the calibration regression $\mathrm{logit}\Pr(y=1)=\alpha+\beta\,\mathrm{logit}(p)$ (ideal: $\alpha=0,\beta=1$).
  • Directional hit rate with a Wilson interval and a binomial test against 50%. Because 15-minute windows issued every 5 minutes overlap, consecutive outcomes are correlated. Under a driftless random walk the variance inflation is $D_{\text{eff}}=1+2\sum_{k=1}^{h-1}\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin(1-k/h)\approx2.36$, so the effective sample size is $n/D_{\text{eff}}$ and all hit-rate intervals use it.
  • Diebold–Mariano test (Diebold & Mariano 1995) on loss differentials $d_t=L^{A}_t-L^{B}_t$: $DM=\bar d/\sqrt{\hat V/n}$ with a Newey–West long-run variance $\hat V=\hat\gamma_0+2\sum_{k=1}^{3}(1-\tfrac k4)\hat\gamma_k$ (Newey & West 1987), the Harvey–Leybourne–Newbold (1997) small-sample correction, referred to $t_{n-1}$. Reported against $p=0.5$ and against climatology.
  • Interval forecasts: empirical coverage of the 50%, 80% and 90% intervals (target equals nominal) and mean pinball loss $\rho_\tau(q,y)=\max\{\tau(y-q),(\tau-1)(y-q)\}$ averaged over the seven quantiles, in basis points.

Threshold-contract (strike ladder) score. The same predictive distribution prices any “above strike $K$ at the target time” question: $\Pr(S>x)=T_\nu(\mu'-x/s)$. Six fixed strikes at $x\in\{-40,-25,-10,+10,+25,+40\}$ bps around the price at issue are scored with the Brier score and compared with per-strike climatology. Because strikes far from spot are easy to call, this number is much lower than the direction Brier and depends entirely on how the strikes are spaced; it is reported separately and never mixed with the direction score. A directional Brier near 0.08 is not achievable on a near-martingale; a ladder Brier in that region only reflects easy strikes.

9. Experimental: transfer entropy (information flow)

Directed information flow from BTC to XRP is measured with transfer entropy (Schreiber 2000) — an information-theoretic measure of how much knowing BTC's past reduces uncertainty about XRP's next move, beyond what XRP's own past already tells you. The exact estimator, binning, and significance procedure are proprietary lab work; what matters for judging it is below.

In plain English: does BTC "talk" to XRP in a way that predicts its next move? Implementation, conceptually:

  • Data: 5-minute log returns of BTC-USD and XRP-USD (Coinbase), timestamps aligned, over a rolling 24-hour window, recomputed every cycle; a minimum bar count is required, otherwise the member abstains.
  • Discretization: within each window, returns are cut into adaptive bins (down / flat / up) — the bin edges follow that window's own distribution, so the symbols always adapt to current volatility.
  • Significance: shuffle-surrogate testing of the source series (shuffling destroys coupling while preserving marginals); flow counts as real only if it clears a fixed significance bar.
  • Vote: a small, capped directional vote following the recent BTC move — only when net BTC→XRP flow is positive and significant; otherwise the member abstains. The cap keeps any single vote small.
  • How it feeds the forecast: blended as a fifth member at a small gated weight (currently zero — scored but not used).
  • Validation: the member's out-of-sample Brier is scored on every forecast and shown against the ensemble Brier on the page. Honest caveat: the literature measures information half-life in milliseconds — at 15-minute horizons this edge may be near zero, and the live scoreboard, not the theory, is the verdict.

10. Experimental: permutation-entropy regime filter

Permutation entropy (Bandt & Pompe 2002) measures how ordered a series is, on a 0–1 scale where 0 means perfectly ordered and 1 means pure noise. When the market crosses into a noise regime, the model distrusts itself: the pooled probability is pulled partway toward 0.5 before the infoflow vote is blended in. The exact threshold and pull strength are proprietary; every engagement and release is written to the agent log.

11. Experimental: escrow calendar

Ripple releases 1B XRP from escrow on the 1st of each month and re-locks 60–90% of it within days — fully predictable, so the price impact is small (historical 7-day swings roughly −3.1% to +1.7%). The model carries a tiny bearish tilt. With $d$ = whole days since the 1st (UTC) and $\rho$ the re-lock ratio (manual input, default 0.75 = historical average):

$$\mathrm{tilt}(d)=0.01\left(1-\frac{d}{6}\right)\cdot\frac{1-\rho}{1-0.75},\qquad d=0,\dots,6,$$

and 0 afterwards: at most 0.01 off $P(\text{up})$ on the 1st, decaying linearly to 0 by the 7th, stronger when the re-lock is lower (more XRP stays out). Applied as $\tilde p\leftarrow\tilde p-\mathrm{tilt}\cdot$escrowWeight (default 0). escrowRelock is a factual manual input the agent is never allowed to tune. Validated out-of-sample by comparing the what-if tilted series against the issued forecast inside the 1st–7th window on the scoreboard.

12. Experimental: macro event calendar

Monthly/quarterly macro levels are step functions — useless at 15-minute horizons — but the release events are scheduled, public, and reliably spike volatility. The surprise direction is a coin flip, so the model responds with humility, not a bet. The schedule lives in data/macro-calendar.json (2026 dates taken from the official Federal Reserve, BLS, Census and ISM calendars; trivially extendable into 2027):

  • Tier 1 (±60 min window): FOMC decisions (2:00pm ET), CPI and nonfarm payrolls (8:30am ET).
  • Tier 2 (±30 min window): PPI, advance retail sales (8:30am ET), ISM manufacturing (10:00am ET).

Inside a window, with $w=\,$macroDamp $\in\{0,0.5,1\}$ (default 0), the $P(\text{up})$ deviation is multiplied by $1-w\,(1-\sigma)$ where $\sigma=0.85$ (tier 1) or $0.92$ (tier 2): at full dampening the deviation shrinks to 85% / 92%. The forecast cone widens by the inverse ($1/0.85\approx1.18$ tier-1): the Student-t scale $s_t$ is multiplied, so every quantile band and the strike ladder widen coherently. Validated by event-window out-of-sample Brier on the page; a visible “event window active” indicator shows when dampening applies.

13. Experimental: on-chain exchange flows (slow regime signal)

A deliberately slow signal from the XRP Ledger. It does not vote on 15-minute windows; it emits a persistent regime bias that hangs in there until the flow regime changes.

  • Watchlist (data/onchain-watchlist.json): a handful of exchange wallets whose labels were verified via explorer APIs before inclusion — never guessed. Balances are read from the public XRPL cluster each cycle; no keys, no chain scanning.
  • Net flow: $F_{24}=\sum(\text{balance}_{\text{now}}-\text{balance}_{24\text{h ago}})$ over exchange wallets from our own balance snapshots (history starts empty and warms up over 24h). Inflow ($F>0$) = distribution/sell pressure building (bearish); outflow = accumulation (bullish).
  • Bias: 24-hour and 7-day net exchange flows are normalized, saturated (large flows can't push the bias past a fixed cap), and smoothed slowly — a deliberately sluggish signal. Inflow (coins moving to exchanges) reads as distribution-like pressure; outflow reads as accumulation. The exact coefficients, saturation levels, and smoothing are proprietary lab work; the bias is applied at a gated weight (currently zero).
  • Whale alerts: any single very large Payment touching a watchlist wallet adds a temporary pulse — negative for large inflows to exchanges (distribution-like), positive for large outflows (accumulation-like), smaller for tracked-whale moves — decaying linearly to zero over 48h, logged to the agent log.
  • Validation, two ways: (1) the standard 15-minute Brier contribution of the what-if series; (2) the 24h directional skill — does $\operatorname{sign}(b_t)$ match the sign of the 24h forward return (taken from the ledger's own later forecasts, so no lookahead)? The 24h read is the meaningful one: on-chain edge plays out over hours to days. If the XRPL API is unreachable the member abstains (bias 0) and the forecast never breaks.

14. Experimental: topological features (persistent homology)

Topology studies shape: what stays the same when you stretch but don't tear. Here the "shape" is the cloud of recent market states, and its loops (1-dimensional holes) are the signal. A persistent loop means the market keeps tracing the same cyclic structure; when the loop structure suddenly reorganizes, the regime is changing. Implementation, exactly as coded in lib/topology.js:

  • Takens embedding: 5-minute log returns $r_t$ become points in $\mathbb{R}^3$, $$x_t=[\,r_t,\;r_{t-\tau},\;r_{t-2\tau}\,],\qquad \tau=2\text{ bars (10 min)},$$ over the trailing 144 points (12h). Takens' theorem says a delay embedding reconstructs the topology of the underlying dynamical system — if the market has cyclic structure, the cloud has loops.
  • Vietoris–Rips filtration: connect two points when their Euclidean distance $\le\varepsilon$, and let $\varepsilon$ grow from 0. An edge's filtration value is its length; a triangle's is its longest edge. H0 (components) is tracked by union-find; H1 (loops) by boundary-matrix reduction over $\mathbb{Z}/2$ — edges processed in filtration order birth loops when they close a cycle, triangles kill them when they fill it. Each loop yields a pair $(b,d)$ = (birth, death).
  • Persistence entropy (H1): with finite lifetimes $\ell_i=d_i-b_i$ and $L=\sum_i\ell_i$, $$\mathrm{PE}=-\frac{\sum_i (\ell_i/L)\,\log(\ell_i/L)}{\log n}\in[0,1],$$ $n$ = number of finite bars. 0 = one dominant loop (ordered); 1 = lifetimes spread evenly (noise).
  • Regime-change distance: the 2-Wasserstein distance between this window's H1 diagram and the previous window's, computed on the lifetime marginals — sort both lifetime multisets descending, pad the shorter with zeros (bars matched to the diagonal), take the L2 distance. Sorting is the optimal 1-D coupling (rearrangement inequality), so this is exact for the lifetime distributions; birth-time information is dropped for speed.
  • Dampening rule: distances accumulate in a rolling history (cap 2016 = 7 days). Once $\ge$ 50 samples exist, the threshold is $\mathrm{median}+2\sigma$; a spike above it means the cyclic structure is reorganizing, and $\tilde p\leftarrow\tfrac12+0.9\,(\tilde p-\tfrac12)$. Applied as a what-if series with topologyWeight (default 0).
  • Experimental nudge (disabled): lib/topology.js also implements a directional nudge — when $\mathrm{PE}<0.4$ and one loop dominates ($\max\ell>3\times$ median), nudge $\pm0.02$ with the sign of the recent 15-minute return (the local phase velocity around the loop). This is a heuristic, not a theorem; it is computed for transparency but never applied by the runner.
  • Validation: the what-if dampened series is scored out-of-sample; the meaningful read is the Brier inside regime-change windows ($\ge$10 needed). Honest caveat: the published XRP-ledger topology work is weekly-horizon anomaly detection on the transaction graph — this is a 15-minute adaptation on the price embedding, and it is unproven. Cost note: the H1 reduction runs ~3s per cycle on 144 points ($\binom{144}{3}=487{,}344$ triangles); it never blocks the forecast (best-effort with graceful skip).

15. How experimental members earn weight

Every experimental member — transfer entropy, escrow calendar, macro dampening, on-chain bias, topological dampening — starts at weight 0: it is computed and scored on every forecast but cannot move the published probability. Promotion happens only through the accuracy agent's challenger gate: a candidate weight is replayed walk-forward over a held-out block of recent bars, and it is adopted only if the Brier improvement clears a minimum effect size, a Diebold–Mariano test with Bonferroni correction over all candidates, and stability across both halves of the block — otherwise the champion is kept and the attempt is logged with the reason. Manual factual inputs (the escrow re-lock ratio) are never tuned by the agent. In short: no theory gets weight without out-of-sample evidence; the scoreboard is the verdict.

8. The accuracy agent: how it improves without deviating

  1. Confidence guard (shrinkage). From live data the agent computes the ridge-regularised optimal shrinkage, the minimiser of $\sum_i(\tfrac12+\lambda(\tilde p_i-\tfrac12)-y_i)^2+\kappa\lambda^2$: $$\lambda^{*}=\operatorname{clip}\!\Big(\frac{\sum_i(\tilde p_i-\frac12)(y_i-\frac12)}{\sum_i(\tilde p_i-\frac12)^2+\kappa},0,1\Big),\quad \kappa=0.2 .$$ The prior $\kappa$ pulls $\lambda$ toward 0, so a model has to earn confidence with roughly a thousand scored forecasts. It is applied only with at least 576 live forecasts, at most once per 24 h, and only if it moves by at least 0.10 (hysteresis). If the live evidence shows no skill, forecasts collapse toward 50% and the Brier score converges to the no-skill 0.25 rather than getting worse.
  2. Champion/challenger search on a regular schedule (earlier if a degradation alarm fires). A set of challengers is generated by small random perturbations of a few hyper-parameters, or by toggling one feature. All are replayed with the production engine over the loaded multi-week history; the final days form the held-out block, and challengers are scored on the pre-shrink probability so that model quality is separated from the confidence guard.
  3. Adoption gate. The best challenger is adopted only if all hold: the Brier improvement clears a minimum effect size; a one-sided Diebold–Mariano test passes a Bonferroni-corrected significance bar across all candidates; and the improvement has the same sign in both halves of the held-out block. At most one adoption per day. Otherwise the champion is kept and the numbers behind that decision are logged.
  4. Live verification and rollback. After an adoption, the previous champion is replayed over the live period and compared with what was actually issued (Diebold–Mariano on live pre-shrink losses). If the previous model would have been clearly better, the agent reverts automatically.
  5. Degradation alarm. If over the last 288 live forecasts the model is worse than climatology by more than $5\times10^{-4}$ in mean Brier ($p<0.1$), an early search is triggered.
  6. Fail-safe. The runner refuses to start if the ledger's hash chain is broken, skips (and records) any bar it cannot forecast on time, and never edits past records.

The agent is deliberately statistical and rule-based: it needs no paid language-model API, every decision is reproducible from the public ledger and seed, and it cannot be talked into a bad change.

9. Integrity

  • The ledger is append-only JSON lines. Each record stores hash = SHA-256(prev_hash ‖ canonical_json(record)); editing any past line breaks every later hash. verifyChain() in lib/io.js checks it, and the head hash is published.
  • Forecasts are issued within 150 seconds of the bar close or skipped and recorded as gaps; the lag of every forecast is stored. Each record also stores the configuration hash and a digest of its input bars.
  • All source code, the configuration history and the ledger are downloadable below.

10. Limits and honest expectations

  • A 15-minute crypto direction forecast built from free price/volume data should be expected to deliver a Brier skill of at most a few tenths of a percent to a few percent versus 0.25, and a hit rate within a point or two of 50%. If the live record shows no significant skill, this page says so and the agent shrinks the forecasts. Claiming more would be a calibration failure.
  • The scores concern probabilistic accuracy of the last-trade price, not trading profit; fees, spread and slippage are ignored.
  • The agent's repeated searches over overlapping data carry a selection risk. The Bonferroni correction, the minimum effect size, the two-half stability rule and the live rollback test mitigate it but cannot remove it, which is why the live ledger, not the replay, is the scoreboard.
  • GitHub Actions schedules are best-effort: a missed bar is recorded as a gap, never invented.
