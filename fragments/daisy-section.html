Derivatives positioning — perpetuals regime signal experimental

Daisy watches the public derivatives feed for XRP perpetuals. Funding is her crowd meter: persistently positive funding means the long side is crowded and fragile (bearish tilt); persistently negative funding means shorts are crowded (bullish tilt). Open interest is the amplifier — a tilt with rising open interest means fresh money piling into the crowded side, which strengthens the read. The member emits a slow positioning tilt — currently at weight zero, so it is scored but never affects the forecast until Daisy finds out-of-sample evidence.

How it works: every cycle the runner reads the funding-rate history and open-interest trail for the XRP perpetual contract from the public exchange API (no keys) and asks one question — is the derivatives crowd packed into one side? The tilt is deliberately slow — it hangs in there across many windows until the positioning regime genuinely changes. Honest caveat: by the time a crowded regime is obvious on the funding board, the market has often already priced the squeeze — the edge, if any, is in persistent regimes the crowd misreads, not a single extreme print.
