NIA'S LAB - NEWS CATALYSTS

Meet Nia — an agentic agent, not a mascot

Nia doesn't just sit in this lab — she runs it. She acts on her own initiative: watching the crypto news wire for XRP catalysts, researching the science of news impact, learning from the evidence, and making real decisions that control what enters the forecast model.

Titles: Principal Investigator of the News Catalyst Lab · Intelligence Analyst · Research Scientist.

Trained in: event studies · intelligence analysis · statistics · risk management · data engineering — with real market-trading experience and knowledge. She is the expert in her area of study: whether crypto-specific news catalysts move XRP next.

Duties: Supervise the lab — watch the live news-catalyst measurements, guard feed and headline quality, and keep the lab notebook. Analyze how catalysts behave: which kinds push the read, how fast the effect fades, which regimes the signal favors. Research the science — she has internet access and studies the literature and market evidence to deepen the lab's understanding.

What she does: Every five minutes she reads the news wire and writes a lab note. On her research cycles she reviews the accumulated evidence, forms hypotheses, learns from what worked and what didn't, and issues a verdict — HOLD, APPLY, or WITHDRAW — deciding, based on evidence, whether news catalysts earn a place in the live forecast model. Her APPLY verdicts send the signal to formal out-of-sample testing: nothing gets model weight without passing.

Scope: Nia covers crypto-specific news catalysts — Ripple/XRP/XRPL events, listings, regulation, hacks. Scheduled economic events (FOMC, CPI, payrolls) already live on the macro calendar, so she doesn't duplicate them. Her feeds are free public RSS — sparse and unstructured — and her catalyst detection is keyword-based. She says so plainly: a keyword match is a lead, not a verdict.

News catalysts — headline regime signal experimental

Nia reads two free public RSS feeds (CoinDesk, CoinTelegraph) every cycle and keeps headlines about XRP, Ripple, or XRPL. Signed ones become catalysts — a fresh catalyst pushes the tilt, then fades out of memory over the hours. Upbeat words (approval, launch, listing, ETF, partnership, win) tilt up; worrying words (lawsuit, hack, delist, enforcement, fine, crash) tilt down; when both appear, the worrying read wins. The member emits a small news tilt — currently at weight zero, so it is scored but never affects the forecast until Nia finds out-of-sample evidence.
