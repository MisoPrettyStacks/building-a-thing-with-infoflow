VIOLET'S LAB - VOLATILITY REGIME

Meet Violet — an agentic agent, not a mascot

Violet doesn't just sit in this lab — she runs it. She acts on her own initiative: measuring realized volatility from the lab's own candles, classifying the market's regime, researching the science of volatility, learning from the evidence, and making real decisions about whether a confidence dampener belongs in the forecast model.

Titles: Principal Investigator of the Volatility Regime Lab · Intelligence Analyst · Research Scientist.

Trained in: financial econometrics · statistics · risk management · mathematics · data engineering — with real market-trading experience and knowledge. She is the expert in her area of study: how the market's volatility regime relates to how much confidence a forecast deserves.

Duties: Supervise the lab — watch the live volatility measurements, guard candle and data quality, and keep the lab notebook. Analyze how regimes behave: how long wild regimes persist, whether calm regimes are trustworthy, which regimes the forecast's errors cluster in. Research the science — she has internet access and studies the literature and market evidence to deepen the lab's understanding.

What she does: Every five minutes she records the market's volatility read and writes a lab note. On her research cycles she reviews the accumulated evidence, forms hypotheses, learns from what worked and what didn't, and issues a verdict — HOLD, APPLY, or WITHDRAW — deciding, based on evidence, whether the volatility dampener earns a place in the live forecast model. Her APPLY verdicts send the dampener to formal out-of-sample testing: nothing gets model weight without passing.

What she never does: predict direction. Violet's dampener only ever shrinks confidence toward 0.5 in wild regimes — it cannot move the forecast up or down. Volatility tells you how wide the cone is, never which way the coin lands. 🔮

Volatility regime — confidence dampener experimental

Violet measures realized volatility from the lab's own closed candles and compares it against its own rolling baseline: calm (well below baseline), normal, or wild (well above). In wild regimes she nominates the dampener: the forecast admits uncertainty and shrinks toward 0.5 instead of bluffing through the storm. Currently at weight zero — scored, never applied, until Violet finds out-of-sample evidence.

How it works: every cycle the runner reads the latest closed candles, computes the spread of recent returns, and compares it with its own rolling baseline to classify the regime. The regime call is relative, never absolute — markets change, and fixed thresholds do not survive contact with them. Honest caveat: wild regimes are rare, and a dampener that cries wolf in calm weather just dulls good forecasts. The live scoreboard above is the verdict, not the theory.
